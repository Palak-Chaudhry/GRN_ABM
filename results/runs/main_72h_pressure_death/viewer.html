Crowding now kills, and the tissue holds its own density

The run starts with 1,000 tumour cells, 1,000 T cells and 1,000 macrophages and steps them for 72 simulated hours on the curated 167-gene network. The final model adds one rule to the earlier run: mechanical pressure raises a cell’s apoptosis rate (half_max 3.0, max 2e‑3/min, Hill 4). Everything else is identical, including the seed. The earlier run, main_72h_curated, is the control throughout this page.

What the death rule changed

Pressure now feeds back on itself. In the control, median pressure climbs for the whole run. With the rule it falls, then holds near 0.8–0.9. Cells above 3 SD of pressure at 72 h drop from 9.3% to 2.3%. The relief also loosens contact inhibition: the median division rate at 72 h is 5.6e‑4/min, against 2.4e‑4/min in the control.

Four things to check before calling it final

The rule does what it was added to do. These four side effects come from the same numbers and are worth a decision.

1 · The rule fires hard at t = 0

Both runs start from the same tissue, which was packed and calibrated with no pressure-death rule. With the rule on, the median apoptosis rate is already 2.4× base at t = 0 (control 1.07×). The first 3 h see 180 death starts against 102. By 9 h, 511 cells are dying, and mean contacts per cell have dropped from 4.47 to 3.33. Contacts then stay at 3.0–3.3 for the rest of the run.

So the first ~12 h are the tissue relaxing to the density the new rule tolerates, not steady-state behaviour. If you compare gene states near t = 0, drop that window. Alternatively, start the rule after the warm-up or ramp it in.

2 · Macrophages decline steadily

Macrophages go 1,000 → 651 live, against 1,148 in the control: 965 deaths and only 616 divisions. Their base division rate is 0, so a lost macrophage is replaced only when the proliferation rule fires. They are also the largest cells (10 µm radius), so they overlap more at the same packing. In the control they had the highest median pressure of any type (1.82, against 1.51 for tumour and 1.15 for T cells).

The new rule applies to every type (cell_type "*"). If crowding death is meant for tumour only, add cell_type: tumor to that row of configs/rules_pressure_death.yaml.

3 · Density regulation, not a necrotic core

Dying cells are spread evenly through the packed body of the tissue and fall off only at the rim. Density drops to 0.82–0.90 of the control in every band, so the tissue thins everywhere instead of hollowing out.

Bands are fractions of the 99th-percentile radius at 72 h (1,021 µm final, 1,042 µm control). Pressure is flat inside the packed region and collapses at its edge. It encodes “inside the tissue” rather than “far from the surface”, so it cannot carve a dead centre. A necrotic core needs a signal like distance from the surface or a diffusing nutrient, which the plugin system can add next to distance_from_centre.

4 · Fewer contacts, less signalling, stronger TF drift

Signalling is by contact, so losing a quarter of the contacts lowers the ligand each cell receives. Per receiving cell at 72 h (plot 14d, read from the chart), T cells are down about 1.1 log2 and macrophages about 0.7. Tumour cells dip to about −0.45 and recover to roughly 0. In the control the same numbers were −0.35, +0.11 and +0.57.

  --rules configs/rules_pressure_death.yaml --hours 72 --snapshot-min 180 \
  --name main_72h_pressure_death
RUN=results/runs/main_72h_pressure_death
sbatch -J analysis  scripts/run_analysis.sbatch $RUN
sbatch -J endpoints scripts/run_py.sbatch scripts/11_export_endpoints.py --run $RUN
